Spicy miso Salmon Onigiri recipe

Description

Riceballs.. it's what is on the inside that counts

Ingredients

  • Furikake Seasoning
  • 2 Filets of Salmon
  • Adobo Seasoning
  • Soy Sauce
  • Sesame oil
  • Chili Oil
  • Garlic powder
  • Spicy Miso Gochujang
  • Ground Ginger
  • Short-grain Sushi rice
  • Small sauce pan w/ lid
  • Rice Vinegar
  • Onigiri molds
  • Dry seaweed wrap
  • Honey
  • Spatula
  • Skillet or Saucepan. Won't be eaten but consider it necesarry to cook these items.

Salmon Preparation

  1. Dress up salmon fillets with adobo, soy sauce, and furikake.
  2. Heat saucepan with chili oil as lubricator
  3. Med heat, grill skin-side down. 7 Minutes.
  4. Med heat, cook tissue-side down. 5 minutes
  5. Use spatula to slice into the fillets. Cook until every piece of meat is chunked into a pool
  6. Add more furikake to taste, drizzle in sesame sauce.
  7. Gochujang spicy miso time. Don't drown it, but add enough for your ideal flavor presence. Top off our combination with honey.
  8. Set aside -- as we begin to work on our rice.

Rice Preparation

  1. Bring 1 1/4 of rice to a sauce pan. Wash until cleaning reps end with water that remains at least mostly clear. Good technique involves squishing grains together and pouring water once it becomes milky
  2. Fill sauce pan halfway with water
  3. High heat, boil for ten minutes
  4. Med heat, partially cover with lid or cover entirely with holed lid. Boil for another ten minutes
  5. Leave it to sit for an additional ten minutes
  6. Add spoonful of rice vinegar, soy sauce, and the smallest amount of honey for seasoning. Mix.
  7. Our rice is finished!

Finalizing our Onigiri

The last little stretch here.. yeesh.

  1. Your onigiri molds should be of two pieces. Take one and fill it with rice
  2. In the center, create something of an impression where your salmon filling will go
  3. Fill the other mold with rice
  4. Hold together for around 30 seconds
  5. Remove our onigiri from the molds
  6. Take a small, six-inch section from the seaweed sheet and wrap it around our riceball. I personally favor the sumo wrestler look but variantions are acceptable
  7. Make all the riceballs your resources allow! Leftover meat can be great for eggs, sandwiches, or salad protein.
  8. Enjoy!
